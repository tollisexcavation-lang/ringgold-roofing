How a roof claim works, step by step

Every insurer and policy is a little different, but most roof claims follow these steps.

  1. Stay safe and stop more damage

    Stay off a damaged roof. If water is getting in, call us for emergency tarping. Keep receipts for tarps or temporary repairs, and take your own photos of damage inside and out. Most policies expect you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage.

  2. Get a free inspection and documentation

    We inspect the roof, gutters, vents, flashing, and soft metals, and document what we find with photos, measurements, and a written summary. If we don't see storm damage, we will tell you that straight, so you don't file a claim you don't need.

  3. Review your policy and file the claim

    Check your deductible (some wind and hail deductibles are a percentage of the home's coverage, not a flat dollar amount) and whether your roof is covered at replacement cost or actual cash value. Then you file the claim with your insurance company or agent and write down your claim number. The claim is yours, between you and your insurer.

  4. The adjuster inspection

    Your insurer sends an adjuster to look at the roof. We can meet the adjuster at your home, walk the roof with them, point out the damage we documented, and share our photos and measurements. Under Georgia law a roofer can't file or negotiate your claim for you unless they are a licensed public adjuster, so we stick to showing the facts of the roof.

  5. Read the scope and estimate

    The insurer sends a written estimate (the “scope”) listing what it will pay for. We go through it line by line with you and compare it to what the roof actually needs, such as tear-off layers, drip edge, starter, ridge, vents, flashing, and any code-required items.

  6. Supplements, if something was missed

    If the scope missed items, or we find hidden damage once the old roof is off (like rotted decking), we document it with photos and measurements and prepare the supplement paperwork so your insurer can review it. Supplements are normal; they're how the estimate gets corrected to match the real job.

  7. ACV, RCV, and depreciation

    Replacement cost value (RCV) is what it costs to replace the roof today. Actual cash value (ACV) is RCV minus depreciation for the roof's age. On many replacement-cost policies, the first check is the ACV minus your deductible, and the held-back “recoverable depreciation” is paid after the work is finished and the final invoice is sent. Some policies pay ACV only on roofs, so check yours, and watch the deadline for claiming depreciation.

  8. Your deductible is yours to pay

    The homeowner pays the deductible. Georgia law (O.C.G.A. § 33-23-43) makes it illegal for a contractor to pay, waive, or rebate any part of your deductible, including disguised “discounts,” credits, or yard-sign allowances. We will never offer that, and if a contractor does, treat it as a red flag. The deductible is paid to us as part of the job price written in your contract.

  9. The work

    Once the scope is agreed and you sign a written contract, we schedule the job, order materials, tear off and inspect the deck, replace bad wood, and install the new roof. When we're done, we clean up the job site.

  10. Final invoice and final check

    We send you the final invoice and completion photos so you can submit them to your insurer to release any recoverable depreciation. If your mortgage company is named on the check, it may need to endorse it or release funds in stages. You pay the balance as written in your contract.

About your deductible

We'd rather be upfront about this than lose your trust later.

Your deductible is part of your insurance contract, and you pay it. In Georgia, it is against the law for a contractor to pay, waive, or rebate any part of your deductible (O.C.G.A. § 33-23-43). That includes “we'll cover your deductible” offers, fake discounts, credits, gift cards, and yard-sign allowances. The Georgia Attorney General's office warns homeowners that these offers can be a sign of fraud.

Tolli's Construction will never offer to waive your deductible. What we will do is give you a clear written price, show you exactly what the insurance scope covers, and make sure the roof is done right.

Red flags after a storm

  • Anyone offering to pay or “eat” your deductible.
  • A “free roof” or “no out-of-pocket” promise.
  • Pressure to sign on the spot, or full payment up front.
  • A roofer saying they will file or negotiate your claim for you.
  • No written contract, or a contractor you can't reach locally.

Roof insurance claim FAQs

Straight answers to the questions homeowners ask us most.

Do I have to file an insurance claim to get a free roof inspection?

No. The inspection is free whether or not you file a claim. We'll tell you honestly whether we see storm damage and whether a claim looks worth discussing with your insurer.

Can Tolli's pay or waive my insurance deductible?

No. Georgia law (O.C.G.A. § 33-23-43) prohibits contractors from paying, waiving, or rebating any part of a homeowner's insurance deductible, including through discounts, credits, or sign allowances. The deductible is the homeowner's responsibility, and we don't waive deductibles anywhere we work.

Can you talk to my insurance company for me?

We can meet your adjuster at the home, walk the roof with them, and share our photos, measurements, and documentation. You are the policyholder and you file the claim. In Georgia, a roofer can't file or negotiate a claim on your behalf unless they are a licensed public adjuster.

What is the difference between ACV and RCV?

Replacement cost value (RCV) is the cost to replace the roof today. Actual cash value (ACV) is RCV minus depreciation for age and wear. Many replacement-cost policies pay ACV first and release the held-back depreciation after the work is completed and the final invoice is submitted.

What if the adjuster misses damage or denies the claim?

Ask the insurer for the decision in writing, ask what it was based on, and ask about a re-inspection. We can provide our documentation, and if work reveals hidden damage we prepare a supplement with photos. For a dispute, you can also talk to your agent, a licensed public adjuster, or an attorney.

Will filing a claim raise my insurance rates?

That depends on your insurer and policy, so ask your agent. We can't promise how a claim affects your premium.

How long do I have to file a roof claim?

It depends on your policy. Many policies require prompt notice after a storm and set deadlines for filing and for collecting recoverable depreciation. Check your policy or ask your agent, and get the roof inspected soon after the storm.

Do you help with claims in Chattanooga and Tennessee too?

Yes. We serve Ringgold, North Georgia, and the Chattanooga area. Policies and state rules can differ, so read your policy carefully, but we never pay or waive deductibles in any state.
